02The problem

Students don’t lack ambition. They lack a plan that survives the week.

01

Scattered

Classes live in the student portal, deadlines in five syllabi, everything else in notes and group chats.

02

Rigid

Hand-made plans break the moment a week changes — so they get abandoned by week three.

03

Heavy

Most planners take longer to set up and maintain than the time they actually save.

Design principles
CalmWarm palette, generous space, one clear primary action per screen.
BalancedStudy time spread across the lightest days, tracked against a weekly goal.
ForgivingUndo everywhere. Conflicts warn instead of block. Nothing is ever lost.

03Who it’s for

Meet Jordan.

Proto-persona · second-year student juggling four classes, a design club, the gym and a part-time life.

“I know what I need to do. I just never know when I’m going to do it.”

Goals

  • Protect real study time every week
  • See today at a glance, between classes
  • Never get surprised by a deadline

Frustrations

  • Generic calendars don’t understand classes vs. study
  • Re-planning after one busy day feels like starting over
  • Apps that need an account before showing any value

Context

  • Plans on a phone, between lectures
  • Checks the week on a laptop on Sunday night

Editing that guides instead of scolds.

13UX details

Small details, big calm.

Value before sign-up

The landing page shows a real week preview; any email opens a sample week.

Forgiving by default

Deletes, moves and completions all come with an Undo toast.

Plain-language errors

“Pick at least one class to continue” beats a red outline every time.

Thumb-zone actions

A raised add button and bottom navigation keep key actions reachable.

Keyboard friendly

⌘K search, Esc to close, Enter to open — with visible focus states.

Private by design

No backend: schedules stay on the student’s own device.
